Every WCAG 2.2 criterion, shown rather than described.

Four principles, thirteen guidelines, and a card for each success criterion: a plain-English restatement, a failing example, and the same interface after it passes. Everything on this page obeys the criteria it documents.

How to read a card

Number and level. Criterion numbers are set in tabular monospace so they align down the page. Badges differ in shape, outline, and label — never hue alone.

Fail, then pass. The left frame is marked ✕ FAIL, the right ✓ PASS. A caption under each names the one thing that changed.

Evidence. Where the point is code, a markup diff sits beneath the visual. Where the point is perceptual, the visual carries it alone.

1

Principle one

Perceivable

If you can't perceive it, it isn't there for you.

Information and interface components must be presentable in ways every user can sense. Text that can be read aloud or enlarged; contrast that survives a bright room; structure that survives losing the layout.

1.4

Distinguishable

Guideline 4 of 4 · Perceivable

Make it easier for users to see and hear content, including separating foreground from background. This is where most audits fail, and where the failures are easiest to see.

1.4.1

Use of Color

■LEVEL A #

In plain English: colour can carry meaning, but never on its own. If removing all colour from the screen would lose information, add a shape, a label, an underline, or an icon.

✕FAIL

Your order ships in two days. See the delivery schedule for regional cut-off times, or read the returns policy.

Links are blue text and nothing else. In greyscale, at low vision, or on a monochrome display they are indistinguishable from the sentence around them.

What's wrong: hue is the only signal.

✓PASS

Your order ships in two days. See the delivery schedule for regional cut-off times, or read the returns policy.

The underline survives greyscale, inverted colour, and a monochrome print-out. Colour still helps; it is no longer load-bearing.

What changed: a persistent underline was added.

1.4.3

Contrast (Minimum)

◐LEVEL AA #

In plain English: body text needs at least 4.5:1 against its background. Text at 24px, or 19px bold and above, needs 3:1. Measure it; don't eyeball it.

✕FAIL

Placeholder grey, helper grey, and disabled grey are three different mistakes with the same cause.

✕ 2.3:1 measured

What's wrong: #9C9C9C on white is 2.3:1 — half the required ratio.

✓PASS

Helper text at #54524C keeps its quieter voice through weight and size rather than through fading out.

✓ 7.4:1 measured

1.4.11

Non-text Contrast

◐LEVEL AA #

In plain English: the parts of a control that tell you it is a control — the input's edge, the checkbox box, the focus ring, the chart key — need 3:1 against what sits behind them.

✕FAIL

✕ border 1.2:1

What's wrong: the field and checkbox edges vanish; only the placeholder hints that anything is editable.
