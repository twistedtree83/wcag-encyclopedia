## Decisions made

**The through-line for the guideline is stated once and demonstrated five ways:** structure has
to survive losing the presentation. If the only thing making a heading a heading is that it is
big and bold, it is not a heading.

**1.3.1's fail and pass examples are visually identical**, deliberately. That is the whole
lesson — nothing on screen changes, and everything underneath does. The diff makes the point
that the CSS need not change either: restyle the real elements to look how the fake ones looked.

**1.3.2's failing example actually reorders itself with flexbox `order`**, rather than merely
describing the problem. The painted sequence reads 1-2-3 while the document reads 3-1-2, which
is the bug exactly as it occurs in real code.

**1.3.3's pass example keeps the colour and position.** The criterion is often over-corrected
into "never mention where something is". "The green Continue button on the left" is fine; what
fails is position and colour being the *only* identification. Saying so avoids teaching a
superstition.

**1.3.4 is cross-referenced to 1.4.10.** Orientation stops being a special case once layout
responds to its container, which is the same discipline Reflow asks for — worth the reader
seeing the connection rather than treating them as two unrelated rules.
