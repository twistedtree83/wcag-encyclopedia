## Decisions made

**The guideline is organised around the moments surrounding a mistake** — before it (3.3.2
labels), during it (3.3.1 identification), after it (3.3.3 suggestion), and instead of it
(3.3.4 prevention). That framing turns six rules into a sequence.

**3.3.1 and 3.3.3 are explicitly paired**, since the boundary is the thing people miss: naming
the problem is Level A, suggesting the correction is AA. 3.3.3 also names the exemption where
suggesting a fix would leak information — a wrong password.

**3.3.8 leads with paste-blocking.** It is the most common real-world violation, the cheapest
possible fix (delete one event handler), and the card notes it is a net *security* gain too,
since blocking paste pushes people toward passwords short enough to memorise. The diff is that
one deletion.

**3.3.4's pass example shows all three mechanisms and says any one suffices**, then argues
reversibility is the kindest because it is the only one that still works after the mistake.

**3.3.7's exemption is stated** — confirming a new password is essential re-entry — because
without it the criterion reads as broader than it is.

**Cross-references land where they help:** 3.3.4 points at 2.5.8, since the mis-tap it protects
against is the one target size exists to reduce.
